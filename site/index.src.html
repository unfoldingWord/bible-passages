Bible Passages

Every passage of the Bible, divided into stories and the short segments a translator records, each with a plain title in the translator's own language. It lets a translator find the place in the story without chapter and verse.

Baseline snapshot
Reviewers wanted

We need people who read Spanish, French or Hindi to review short Bible section titles for Titus. Two AI models each translated the 33 English Titus titles into 9 languages. Before we choose which model translates the other 65 books, a person who reads the language needs to check them: keep a title as it is, or change it, and note roughly how long it took. You will not be told which model wrote which title.

For each language that is 66 short titles (33 from each model). Saved so far (30 Oct): English and Portuguese, from one reviewer. A Hindi reviewer says they finished, but no Hindi answers were saved. If that is you, please open the review page again and use “Copy my answers”. If you can help, or know someone who reads Arabic, Chinese, Indonesian, Russian or Swahili and could, reply on the Zulip thread 86 Servant.Bible › Need: Bible Passages dataset and we will send you the review page. The plan is in #64.

Where it stands
  • Titus is built end to end on main: structure (8 pericopes, 25 segments), English segment titles, key-term glossaries in 9 languages, and a Chinese (zh-Hans) key-term list drafted by one model and checked by another, waiting for a Chinese reader (#51). The release path passed a dry run on GitHub (#35); no release has been tagged yet (checked 30 Oct).
  • All 66 books have their structure on main: 3,016 pericopes and 13,770 segments, both counts re-derived by a second script and counted again from the tree on 30 Oct (#40, #37). Glossaries are built for the 59 books with released word links; the other 7 wait for a release (#62, #57).
  • Philippians is the second sample book. Its English segment titles are drafted by one model and checked by another: 23 agree and 14 are disputed, still waiting for a reviewer on 30 Oct (#67, #32).
  • Titus is translated by both models (#58, measured 29 Oct): 297 titles each, no automatic-check failures. The other model disagreed with 10 of Gemini 3.8 Flash's translations and 5 of Claude Sonnet 5.5's. Gemini's translation calls cost about half as much per title. The choice rule counts translating plus the other model's check; on that count Claude's titles cost less (about $0.0021 against $0.0025 a title, the sum of two rows on the comparison page). Cost decides only on a near tie. Quality comes first, measured as reviewer changes (ADR 0002), so the choice waits on the review above. On 30 Oct only one reviewer's answers were saved (#64).
  • Decisions are recorded (ADR 0003): v0.1 is a gated subset; pericopes come from BSB; segment titles are explanatory comments; single-segment pericopes reuse their title (16,523 English titles); v0.1 metadata is key terms, figures of speech and anything already in the data (sizing in #44). Keys are spans and never move once released (ADR 0001). Release assets are named titles.<lang>.<BOOK>.json, and translated titles must pass full-stop and quotation-mark checks in all 9 languages (#87, #81). Open: decision 8 (chunk source), the content team's call, with no answer on #4 as of 30 Oct. See Decisions.
  • A person's review now reaches the data (30 Oct). A reviewed key term is recorded in its own record, and a changed headword is kept like a changed title (#124, #136; decided on #53). apply.ts turns the answers from the review page into one change to data/drafts/ for a pull request (#137, #141). No real review has gone through it yet. The site's own Review tab is dropped; review runs on the separate review page (#49 closed 30 Oct). 24 pull requests merged on 30 Oct (#100 to #143), most of them follow-ups from review.
  • Open Bible Stories comes after v0.1, in this repo, reusing the same pipeline (#78). Five questions for the requirements owner are on the Decisions tab as O1 to O5; no answers were recorded on #78 as of 30 Oct.

    Rates per title

    • Drafting: measured 29 Oct on Philippians, not Titus as first planned. $0.0021 per English segment title, drafting and checking together; about 2,000 titles an hour of model time. All 66 books at that rate: about $28 (estimated). See sizing.md.
    • Translation: measured 29 Oct on Titus, 297 titles per model (#58). Translating plus the other model's check: $0.0025 a title for Gemini 3.8 Flash and $0.0021 for Claude Sonnet 5.5. Not yet scaled to the 148,707 strings of v0.1 (16,523 English titles × 9 languages).
    • Review minutes per title: not measured yet (30 Oct). At an assumed 10 seconds, all of v0.1 is about 459 hours (46 per language). The blind review collects review time (#64); it is read when the review is unblinded.
    Decisions · pick, say why, and the decider records it

    Decisions before data

    Pick the option you'd choose and add a reason. A pick is input; only a recorded decision counts. The requirements owner decides product questions, the content team decides the chunk source, and the DRI decides delivery questions. T1–T6 are Titus segment titles where the checking model disagreed with the drafting model (PR #16); the other 19 passed the check. The requirements owner answered 1, 2, 9 to 14 and T1 to T6 on 28 Oct, and confirmed 11 and T1 on 29 Oct (ADR 0003). The DRI decided 3 and 6 (ADRs 0001 and 0002). Each card says where its decision is recorded; all are in docs/decisions. Still open on 30 Oct: 8 (the content team's, #4), and O1 to O5 on Open Bible Stories (#78), which start after v0.1 ships.

    Decided on 30 Oct by the maintainer (not cards on this page; each is recorded in a comment on its issue): a person's review of a key term is kept in the same record, and a changed headword is kept like a changed title (#53); a heading that starts at verse 1 also gets the psalm superscription's key terms (#91); private-use language tags are not yet allowed in release file names (#92); the README link to this site stays (#86); the site's Review tab is dropped for the review page (#49); both metadata sizing scripts are kept (#127).
